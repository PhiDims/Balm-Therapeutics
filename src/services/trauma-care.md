helpsTitle: Approaches with strong evidence behind them
helps:
  - title: Stabilisation and grounding
    text: Skills to calm your body and come back to the present when memories hit. This always comes first.
  - title: Trauma-focused CBT
    text: Understanding how the trauma changed your thoughts about yourself and the world, and gently facing what you've been avoiding.
  - title: EMDR
    text: Helping the brain process painful memories so they feel like the past, and lose their power to overwhelm you.
  - title: Somatic awareness
    text: Noticing where stress lives in your body, and learning ways to release it.
  - title: Play-based work for children
    text: Younger children work through trauma in play, with their parents supported alongside.
stepsTitle: Healing happens in stages
stepsIntro: We follow a phased approach used in trauma care worldwide. Each stage builds on the one before, and we only move on when you're ready.
stepLabel: Stage
steps:
  - title: Safety and stability
    text: We make sure you are safe now, and build skills to steady your body and mind. You don't have to tell the whole story at this point.
  - title: Working through the memories
    text: When you feel ready, and only then, we work gently with what happened, using trauma-focused CBT or EMDR.
  - title: Moving forward
    text: Rebuilding routines, relationships and hope, and making sense of what happened in a way you can live with.

faqs:
  - q: Do I have to talk about what happened?
    a: Not at the start, and never before you are ready. Early sessions focus on helping you feel safer. When you do talk about it, you choose how much to share.
  - q: It happened years ago. Can therapy still help?
    a: Yes. Many people come to us years after abuse, loss or violence. The effects of trauma can be worked through at any age.
  - q: Can you help my child after an attack or a death?
    a: Yes. We work with children through play and age-appropriate trauma therapy, and support parents alongside so you know how to help at home.
  - q: Is what I share confidential?
    a: Yes, within the limits that apply to all our work. If you tell us a child is being harmed now, we will need to act to protect them, and we will talk with you about how.
  - q: What if I feel worse after a session?
    a: Strong feelings can come up. Your therapist will help you settle before you leave and give you tools for the days in between. Tell us, and we will adjust the pace.
